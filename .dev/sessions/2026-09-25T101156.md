## Categories local to one resource are future scope

An owner will be able to create a category that exists only in their resource, so the shared list
holds only what studies share. A category is its name and its scope, with no display name, and the
name carries the kind of scope as a prefix, `global.` or `resource.`, since every grant, token entry
and audit record already names its resource. Names are never changed. A local category only ever
adds a restriction, so it waits on the conjunction for a record carrying more than one category;
restricting local categories to new values of a shared category's field was rejected because it
makes that field prescriptive. Where a local category's mapping lives is open and is settled first.
Recorded in the roadmap's Future scope, with detail in `docs/atlas/roadmap/local-categories.md`.

## Category names carry their scope from the first release

The prefix is not deferred with local categories. The first release names every category
`global.<name>`, in the payload type, the token examples across the design documents and all 92
category keys of the conformance corpus, and the schema's category row carries the resource it
belongs to, or none for a global one. A version 1 bridge accepts a `resource.` entry, lets it reach
nothing, and records it as the new audit event `category.unenforced` with the reason
`notImplemented`, which gives the audit path an event the first release can exercise; a bare name or
unknown prefix makes the payload malformed and it is treated as not received. Dropping covers the
principal's grant only, so no resource may define a local category while a bridge serving it cannot
enforce one. Only an admin creates a global category, and an owner's `category.create`,
once local categories exist, is bounded to their resource. Reader-facing documents keep "platform"
and unprefixed names.

## Who holds a grant can follow a rule

Recorded in decisions.md beside the baseline decision, and reflected in the tiers, the calculation's
first step and the token exchange event. The baseline was already a rule; the second gives every
signed-in principal the registered grant. A rule decides who holds a grant, never which records a
category covers; the controller evaluates it at the exchange so the bridge sees an ordinary grant; it
reads only claims the identity provider verifies; and the exchange records which rules applied,
since a rule-conferred grant has no grant row. Case 3 in token-calculation.md now says it matches the
anonymous case only because no resource there carries "registered". Which rules to offer beyond
signing in stays research in the atlas.

Cases 28 to 34 in token-calculation.md cover the rule with and without a sign-in, against a second
configuration so the first 27 keep their answers, and the conformance corpus carries payloads for 28,
29, 30 and 32 under `"configuration": "signed-in rule"`. Writing them found that step 3 would have
dropped a rule-conferred grant, since it has no decision row, so `registered` joins `open` as exempt
from acceptance. Case 33, a revoked principal, is a refusal and belongs to the bridge's tests. Three
questions are open: whether a signed-in principal holds the open grant when the baseline is off
(case 31), whether an expired identity token is refused or served as anonymous (case 34), and whether
a resource may carry "registered" with a restricting category.

## Records not covered by any category are `unmarked`, and open is who reaches them

Removing "open" from a resource left its uncategorized records covered by no category, so no grant,
not even an administrator's self-grant, could reach them. Every resource now has an implicit
`global.unmarked` category for those records, and open is no longer a category: a setting on the
resource says who reaches its unmarked records, open through the baseline, registered through the
signed-in rule, or by grant. That separates which records from who reaches them, as the rule decision
requires, and it answers two of the three questions above: an empty baseline reaches nobody on an
open resource, and a resource set to registered may carry a restricting category. Case 34 stays open.

Every `global.open` key became `global.unmarked` across the tokens, the corpus and the design
examples. decisions.md's "open is the default category" decision became "Records not covered by any category
are `unmarked`", with the settings in its companion decision; the calculation's configuration, step 1,
the acceptance exception and the case rows follow, with a new case 19b and case 35 for records reached
by grant, and case 31 answered in the corpus. The onboarding document's categories sections were
rewritten so the three tiers say who reaches a resource's unmarked records. The atlas note on
categories that select now records that its core is the decided overlay category. Prose elsewhere in
the design corpus still treats `open` as a category, owed as a sweep.

## Relative clauses run subject first, and the prose check enforces it

An object relative clause, "records that a category marks", runs object, subject, verb, and with the
"that" dropped it gives no signal that a clause has started. The fix is the subject-first form,
"records marked by a category", not a restored "that". `check-prose.sh` gained a check over this
model's head nouns and verbs, tuned so a determiner and a noun must precede the verb and indirect
questions pass, and every hit across the corpus was rewritten subject first, including the
`unmarked` decision's heading, now "Records not covered by any category are `unmarked`". The rule is
recorded in the terminology usage rules.

The check missed "the one category nothing marks" on the onboarding page, since it required a
determiner and a noun as the clause's subject. A second check now covers "nothing" or "nobody" as
the subject with singular heads as well; it found that sentence and one table row in
`token-calculation.md`, both rewritten subject first. Other pronoun subjects, "the grants they hold",
stay unmatched, and so do singular heads with a determiner subject, which match 47 sentences.

first release" gains an item for them: created by a dataset's owner, able only to narrow access, and
waiting on records that belong to more than one category. The page keeps prose names, "platform" and
"local", rather than the `global.` and `resource.` prefixes. The local-categories atlas note records
what the page says and what shipping still owes it.
