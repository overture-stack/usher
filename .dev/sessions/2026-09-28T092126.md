# 2026-09-28

## The tool review is checked against current sources, and three platforms join it

Every claim about another tool in decisions.md's "Tools reviewed before building" and in
`docs/why-usher.md` was checked against the projects' own documentation and releases on this date,
and the sources are listed at the end of the landscape section. What changed:

- Cerbos holds no grants and notifies no application of a change; besides its ORM adapters it has an
  unpublished Java adapter for Elasticsearch, and nothing targets SQON. Cerbos Hub has been available
  on premises since January 2026, at the enterprise tier, and its interface authors policies rather
  than recording grants.
- Open-source OPA compiles residuals into SQL or UCAST filters since v1.9.0, with no Elasticsearch
  target, and it keeps its data current by pulling, refined by long polling and delta bundles. Its
  original developers moved to Apple in August 2025; it stays CNCF graduated.
- Keycloak's two open questions are answered from its documentation: it can list every permission a
  user holds, for resources registered in it, but produces no filter, and it cannot delegate part of
  one resource server's policies. The decision still rests on provider independence.
- The relationship stores return identifiers, not filters, and OpenFGA checks no write against its
  model, so what stops a custodian granting themselves access belongs to the administering layer.
  Auth0 FGA has no Canadian region; Permit.io's control plane is available on premises at its
  enterprise tier; Oso's open-source library has been deprecated since December 2023.

A new section, "Comparable platforms in biomedical data sharing", records Gen3, Apache Ranger,
CanDIG v2, and REMS, DUOS and GA4GH Passports as upstream of enforcement. Gen3 is reviewed to learn
from rather than to adopt: Arborist's map of reachable resources, applied by Guppy as a filter over
Elasticsearch, is the nearest existing match to the first release, and its minimum count on
aggregations is the small-cell threshold the first integration does not apply. No system found
filters records within a dataset consistently across several applications.

The case for building is now its own heading, with a table of what exists and what is left to Usher
for each of its four requirements. `docs/why-usher.md` drops its two self-contradictions (Cerbos as
allow or deny only, and relationship stores unable to list what a principal reaches), gives the
Keycloak entry its deciding reason, frames Usher as the layer around a decision engine, and gains
entries for the same platforms.

## Who may revoke, and a custodian can allow an owner to act on their category

The onboarding page named revocation only for administrators. It now says an owner can grant and
revoke within their dataset, a custodian within their category, and an administrator anywhere,
matching admin-model.md and permissions-model.md.

A new decision records how an owner and a custodian meet on one dataset. The custodian's grant stays
the final gate for every request on their category, the owner's included, and a custodian may allow
one dataset's owner to manage that category's access there, withdrawable, and never for the category
everywhere. Custodians appointed per study with shared control, and custodians with exclusive
authority and no way to share it, were weighed and rejected. Three questions stay open for after
MVP: a revoke-only allowance, revoking without an allowance for cause, and grants issued under a
withdrawn allowance. The decision is marked as a starting position for consultation with the
communities concerned, and it assumes categories are cut per community. The page's owner and
custodian rows, the paragraph on where their authority meets, and the Custodian and Revocation
entries reflect it.

## Artifacts can be counted

`artifact.count` joins the vocabulary and the `read` group on an artifact is now `count`, `view` and
`export`, as on a record or a field. It counts artifacts without seeing them, which is what `count`
is on a record; counting the records inside a set stays `record.count` with the set as the filter,
and a set's size is seen with `artifact.view`. The artifact row now lists its actions in the record
row's order of increasing reach, in `permissions-model.md`, the `ArtifactAction` type in
`security-workflow.md` and the fixture checker's vocabulary; decisions.md and the glossary describe
the group the same way. No fixture carried an artifact read, so the corpus is unchanged and passes.
