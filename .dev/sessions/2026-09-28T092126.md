# 2026-09-28

## The tool review is checked against current sources, and three platforms join it

Every claim about another tool in decisions.md's "Tools reviewed before building" and in
`docs/why-usher.md` was checked against the projects' own documentation and releases on this date,
and the sources are listed at the end of the landscape section. What changed:

- Cerbos holds no grants and notifies no application of a change; besides its ORM adapters it has an
  unpublished Java adapter for Elasticsearch, and nothing targets SQON. Cerbos Hub has been available
  on premises since January 2026, at the enterprise tier, and its interface authors policies rather
  than recording grants.
- Open-source OPA compiles residuals into SQL or UCAST filters since v1.9.0, with no Elasticsearch
  target, and it keeps its data current by pulling, refined by long polling and delta bundles. Its
  original developers moved to Apple in August 2025; it stays CNCF graduated.
- Keycloak's two open questions are answered from its documentation: it can list every permission a
  user holds, for resources registered in it, but produces no filter, and it cannot delegate part of
  one resource server's policies. The decision still rests on provider independence.
- The relationship stores return identifiers, not filters, and OpenFGA checks no write against its
  model, so what stops a custodian granting themselves access belongs to the administering layer.
  Auth0 FGA has no Canadian region; Permit.io's control plane is available on premises at its
  enterprise tier; Oso's open-source library has been deprecated since December 2023.

A new section, "Comparable platforms in biomedical data sharing", records Gen3, Apache Ranger,
CanDIG v2, and REMS, DUOS and GA4GH Passports as upstream of enforcement. Gen3 is reviewed to learn
from rather than to adopt: Arborist's map of reachable resources, applied by Guppy as a filter over
Elasticsearch, is the nearest existing match to the first release, and its minimum count on
aggregations is the small-cell threshold the first integration does not apply. No system found
filters records within a dataset consistently across several applications.

The case for building is now its own heading, with a table of what exists and what is left to Usher
for each of its four requirements. `docs/why-usher.md` drops its two self-contradictions (Cerbos as
allow or deny only, and relationship stores unable to list what a principal reaches), gives the
Keycloak entry its deciding reason, frames Usher as the layer around a decision engine, and gains
entries for the same platforms.

## Who may revoke, and owners and custodians act without each other

The onboarding page named revocation only for administrators. It now says an owner can grant and
revoke within their dataset, a custodian within their category, and an administrator anywhere,
matching admin-model.md and permissions-model.md.

A new decision, "An owner governs the whole resource, and a custodian governs their category",
records that neither needs the other's approval: an owner acts on every category of their own
resource, a custodian's included, and a custodian on their category wherever it appears. An
administrator's self-grant still needs the custodians of the data it reaches. A gate on owners, and
a custodian allowing an owner to act one dataset at a time, were weighed and set aside for now, and
either can be adopted later without restructuring. The decision records what it accepts, that an
owner can release community-governed data without the community's approval, constrained by the
custodian seeing and revoking every grant on their category, rate watching and audit, and marks it
as the position most in need of testing in consultation. It also records an open hole: an
administrator who transfers a resource's ownership to themselves holds an ungated owner's authority.
The granting decision, the permissions model, the local-categories note and the page's owner and
custodian rows, the paragraph on where their authority meets, the local-categories reasoning and
the Custodian and Revocation entries follow.

## The entities are a fixed list in code

The `entities` table, added when capabilities became entity and action pairs so that the plane was
stored once per entity, is gone. A row could never add an entity: a data-plane one changes what a
token carries, and a control-plane one is new API. So the list now lives beside the payload type in
`security-workflow.md`, as `DataEntity` (the keys a category entry can carry), `ControlEntity` and
their union `Entity`, and an entity's plane is which union it belongs to. `capabilities` carries
`entity` from that list in place of `entity_id`. Entities carry no stored label: an instance's word
for one, such as "Study" for `resource`, is instance vocabulary, kept out of Usher's schema by the
decision on domain terms, which `management-ui.md` had contradicted and now follows. The first
release has eleven tables of four kinds, plus `invitations` and the two stubs; the sentence counting
them now names the stubs. The sentence saying a service's new capability "costs a row" now says a
new action is added to the vocabulary and the payload type together with the enforcement serving
it. Updated in `permissions-model.md`, `decisions.md`, `management-ui.md` and `phase-1.md`.

## A resource is registered, and a grant names its role

`resource.register` is now the only name for establishing a resource: the stale `resource.create`
in admin-model.md (twice) and permissions-model.md is gone, and the plane example in
permissions-model.md, decisions.md and the glossary now uses `record.update` against
`resource.update`, since the two no longer share a verb. A new decision, "A resource is registered,
not created", records why: a resource is a field and value the data already carries, the verb stays
distinct from `record.create`, and it fits a resource registered before its data arrives.
`role.assign` is gone from admin-model.md, whose example list now points at the settled vocabulary.

`roles` gains a `description` column, empty in the first release and never what a grant is read by.
The paragraph that had a grant record the permissions it carries now says a grant names a role,
the role expands to capabilities when the token is written, and storing capabilities per grant was
rejected because a later role change would leave old grants holding the old set. An invitation lapses seven
days after `invited_at`, needing no column for it, and its `expires_at` is the expiry of the grant it
would create, carried onto that grant when claimed; how a link is verified is recorded as not yet
designed. Grants keep their expiry in the first release.

## A DAC API is expected as an ushered application, and resources would carry a type

Recorded as research in a new atlas note, `docs/atlas/roadmap/dac-integration.md`. A data access
committee layer is its own ushered application, so Usher stores no signed documents, no applicant
details and no DAC interface API; the DAC service asks Usher to register a study and write its grants
once an application is approved. The note traces the study onboarding flow, sets out the data access
flow beside it, and records that resources would need a type: to say which ushered applications
serve a resource, which the controller is said to know and the schema does not hold; to refuse
grants that make no sense on a resource; and to bound which resources a service account registers.
The name was checked against DCAT 3 and Dublin Core, where `dcterms:type`, "the nature or genre of
the resource", is exactly this, so the aligned word is type rather than kind. The note also carries
the service-assigned ownership threat already warned of in admin-model.md, the new `application`
entity as a payload version, and REMS as the existing system to weigh before building one. The
permissions model's expectation that DAC requests would be control-plane entities in Usher, the
matching line in `docs/why-usher.md`, and the roadmap's DACO entry now point at the new direction,
and the atlas index lists the note.

Resource type values name what a resource is, never the service serving it, since one dataset is
served by several services and a type per service would register it once per service. Overture
seeds the vocabulary for every platform, as it seeds roles: `dataset`, DCAT's `dcat:Dataset`, in the
first release, and `access-committee` with the DAC integration, whose applications are records of
that resource rather than resources of their own. `study` was rejected as one platform's word and
`files` because a dataset's files belong to the dataset. An applicant reaching only their own
applications needs principal-relative selection, now recorded as a prerequisite of the integration.
The onboarding document keeps "dataset" for every resource, since it will not cover the DAC.

## Self-grant approval waits, and resources carry their tier and suppression

**Self-grants.** A system administrator is control-plane only, so a self-grant reaching a
resource's data needs the approval of that resource's owner or of a custodian of a category the data
carries, and never of the grantee. The first release has no approval step: self-grants are recorded,
flagged and time-limited, and because every self-grant expires within its limit, adding approval
later leaves none to migrate. The case of a resource with no available approver is recorded as open,
and the earlier stance that an empty approver set is satisfied is not carried forward. The
decision is renamed "Admin self-grant is approved by the data's owner or custodian", and the
granting decision, admin-model.md's mechanics and to-discuss.md follow.

**Resources.** `resources` gains `access_tier` (open, registered or by grant), copied from the
platform's default at registration so that changing the default touches only later resources, and
`suppressed`, set by the new `resource.suppress` and cleared by `resource.restore`, with the audit
events `resource.suppression` and `resource.restoration` replacing `resource.setVisibility` and
`resource.visibilityChange`. Owners and administrators may suppress; a custodian may not, since a
resource holds more than their category. What the open and registered tiers grant stays runtime
configuration. "Visibility policy, private or public" is gone from the corpus, replaced by the tier.
`created_by` and `created_at` are `registered_by` and `registered_at`.

**No category version.** A change to a resource's categories clears the controller's whole payload
cache, and the category version on `resources` is rejected as an optimization ahead of any measured
cost; it also leaves the cache's undefined retention policy nothing to reconcile. A bridge missing
the announcement still meets a cleared cache at its next refresh. The fast-path decision, the
announcement flow, the architecture, security-workflow, glossary, phase-1 and concepts documents and
the roadmap follow.

The tier column is `base_tier`, defined as the least access a resource's records have rather than a
description of the resource: unmarked records sit at it and every category adds a restriction on top.
A resource with no available approver keeps its grants until a system administrator assigns a new
owner, with no further approval in the first release, and the new owner accepts the ownership grant
as usual; the no-owner invariant in permissions-model.md says the same. The ownership-transfer item
is narrowed to its one remaining case, an administrator naming themselves owner, with a proposed
rule that an administrator may assign any owner except themselves.

## Backwards-syntax clauses are rewritten subject first, and the corpus is swept

**The rule.** A noun described by a clause with its own subject, so the noun comes first and the
verb acting on it last, is a backwards-syntax clause; the name avoids "object", which this corpus
already overloads. It is rewritten when the clause's subject is a noun phrase or a quantifier
pronoun, and left alone when it is a personal pronoun. The "that" form, the for-to form and the
predicate relative count; indirect questions, where/when/why/how/whose clauses and prepositional
relatives do not. The rewrite order is a possessive, a participle after the noun, a compound
modifier, then turning the sentence round. An explanation the clearer sentence makes redundant is
deleted; one adding a reason, limit, exception or constraint on a later edit stays. Time and reason
nouns follow the same logic: "the moment X" becomes when, once or as soon as, and "the reason X"
becomes why. Recorded in terminology-usage.md, with the marker for a case that is truly unavoidable.

**The check.** `check-prose.sh` carries two patterns, one over this model's head nouns and verbs in
singular and plural, one over quantifier-pronoun subjects, with a `no longer` exclusion and the
allow marker; the test file covers catches, near-misses, the that-form, the personal-pronoun
near-miss, the marker and `no longer`. The head-noun list is local to this project and the verb list
is portable. A dependency parse finds the general case at higher precision and is an editing aid
only, not part of the check.

**The sweep.** Every markdown file outside the instruction files and session logs was swept. The
prose check now reports no matches across 51 files; the parser's remaining candidates are indirect
questions, subject relatives, complement clauses and quoted text. Headings were kept except the DCAT
decision, renamed "Where DCAT defines a word used by Usher, DCAT's meaning governs", with its one
quotation updated. Paragraphs the sweep touched were rewrapped to 100 characters where the file
wraps.

**Found by the sweep and reconciled.** security-workflow.md still described the refresh as comparing
category versions, and its revocation section compared `generatedAt` against `revoked_at`; both now
state the deletion rule. concepts.md's fast-path paragraph had the same leftover. In decisions.md,
the granting decision's vacancy section still treated a category with no custodian as ungated; it
now says the resource's owner governs it alone and the vacancy removes the community's check rather
than a gate, the vacancy table is remedied rather than directional, the self-ownership exception is
closed, and audit-events.md's `grant.unguarded` note follows. "The version bump stays" is replaced
by the cleared cache as the backstop. Three committed uses of "permission" meaning a feature are
corrected, including the wider-landscape heading. The onboarding audit's application paragraph had
lost its opening clause, and terminology-usage.md's cross-references pointed the wrong way after its
sections were reordered.
