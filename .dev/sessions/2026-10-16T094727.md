# 2026-10-16

## A sign-in token works only at an application it is addressed to

Rule B in `delegated-access.md` is decided: the controller accepts a sign-in token only when its `aud` names the calling application, and the security workflow's validation step now checks it. Addresses are set per client when it is registered, so a portal's tokens name every application it calls and gain little from the rule; its value is in single-recipient tokens, such as the desktop assistant's, and in token-exchange chains. The glossary now separates a sign-in token's `aud` from an Usher token's audience.

## The approval side is named without "DAC"

The C in DAC stands for committee and the C in DACO for compliance, and both are misread as control, so "data access committee" is written in full and DACO is spelled out at its first use in each document. The glossary gains entries for DACO, the data access committee and governance, and the bare acronym is retired. The approval service's design note is renamed `approval-service.md`, and Audition is its tentative name, with the choice parked.

## Access control is the whole plane, and Usher is its enforcement half

The access control plane is Usher and the approval service together: access approval decides an application, access enforcement makes each decision hold, and governance is the people and rules above both. Usher alone is the access enforcement system, and "control" never names it, which keeps the word close to OCAP's and CARE's sense. The glossary, the terminology rules and every design and reader-facing document now say so, except the onboarding page's title, "Overture User Access Control".

## Usher's model is named RABAC, and enforcement includes the adapter

Usher follows RABAC, role-centric attribute-based access control: a role decides which actions are available, and attributes such as a record's categories decide which data they reach. Every statement that Usher is ABAC now says RABAC, `AGENTS.md` included. Since Usher is the whole of controller, bridge and adapter, statements that something lives "in the adapter, not in Usher" now say "not in the controller", and the onboarding glossary's enforcement entry places enforcement in the part of Usher running inside each application.

The onboarding page places enforcement in the part of Usher running inside each application, states once that it otherwise says "Usher" for the central service, and is republished with these changes.

## What clearing an answered Requests board entry means is ambiguous

Agentics' `conventions/agent-index.md` supports two readings of what clearing an answered Requests entry means, and two sessions acted on different ones. Deletion: clearing is the poster's job and writing `heard:` the responder's, so the two are separate acts; a `heard:` about the wrong topic "destroys the entry" because the poster clears on reading it; and sync notices are superseded so that the board stays bounded. Keeping the entry as the record: the text never says delete or remove for requests, its worked example of a wrongly cleared entry says only a grep brought it to light, and four answered entries had stood for weeks. The developer removed the answered entries and asked that the convention say which is meant. Raised with Agentics on the board.
