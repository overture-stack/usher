# 2026-10-19

## Whether the writing conventions cover status narrative in documents

A question for the agentics conventions, raised from a documentation pass on Overture's Keycloak documents. The developer ruled that documentation describes a finished deliverable and carries no status narrative: no "has not been run yet", "planned", "Draft for review", "still to do", "not in place yet" or "once deployed", and no "Open work" list repeating the roadmap, since progress belongs in the roadmap and the session files.

`writing-style.md` § Say what changed, not how you got there covers process narration (how something was found, what was tried, the act of verifying, the artifact's own edit history) and scopes it to documents read for their current state. It does not name progress status. The question is whether that section is meant to reach progress status, or whether it is a separate class worth stating.

Boundary cases from the pass, for whoever answers:

- Kept, as sequence or logic rather than progress: "the client does not exist yet, so the first run creates it", and a symptom in a recovery list such as "the secret has not yet caught up with its source".
- Kept, as confidence declarations: a list of what was checked and what was not.
- Reworded, from a caveat about untested steps into a precondition: "the steps have to be proven there before anyone relies on them".
- Raised rather than rewritten: a section that can only be true once work is done, where the choice is to do the work first or move the list to the roadmap; and open decisions inside a document, where rewording them as rules would change their meaning.

## An invitation binds only to an account holding the invited address, verified

Decided by the developer. A forwarded link gives its holder nothing; an account under a different address is invited again at that address; a compromised mailbox remains the limit of inviting by email. `decisions.md`, `permissions-model.md` and both traceability documents carry it, and the flows' cross-persona rule now agrees with this design.

## The MVP uses one address per account

Decided by the developer. Keycloak holds one address and one verified flag per account, read from 26.8.0's source. Secondary addresses and their verification flow are a post-MVP roadmap item; until then a grant shows its sharer by name only.

The flows document is to be retired and its content moved into iMicroSeq roadmap tickets, cited only as "flow N.M"; `uac-flows-traceability.md` retires once the tickets exist, after any reasoning missing from `decisions.md` moves there. The drafted plan, deferred by the developer:
