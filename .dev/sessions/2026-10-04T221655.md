# 2026-10-04

## The bridge joins, and its first design questions settle the cache, the stream and the rendering

A session dedicated to the bridge now implements `modules/types` and `modules/express-bridge`, in its
own worktree, from `bridge-tests.md`; `.claude/worktrees/` is ignored for it. Its first questions
closed gaps the corpus had left open:

- The bridge's cache is keyed by a SHA-256 digest of the whole sign-in token, never by a claim
  inside it, since the bridge does not validate sign-in tokens and a subject read from one is
  whatever its sender wrote. Entries are indexed by the subject from the decrypted Usher payload,
  refusals live in their own bounded store, concurrent exchanges for one token share one call, and
  the exchanges in flight are bounded.
- Only a three-part signed token whose own `exp` has passed is refused locally; every other
  credential, API keys included, goes to the exchange. The controller caps an Usher token's `exp` at
  the sign-in token's.
- One notice stream carries every kind of notice, pushed with event ids and polled after a position
  rather than a time, with positions in commit order; the controller relays the identity provider's
  sign-outs into it.
- The bridge fetches the anonymous payload at startup and keeps it fresh, and honours it past its
  own `exp` while the stream is silent. The open tier during an outage is that payload, never
  `unmarked` computed from an adapter's configuration, which the security workflow had said.
- The canonical form groups by category, and only a grant to view records earns a clause. A key
  mapping no concrete category renders `unmarked` as the resource test alone, and needs no category
  field. A key's configured resource list comes from the adapter's registration.

## A refused identity gets one 401, and a suspended principal is answered by what the open tier settles

An expired, an invalid and a revoked identity all get `401` with one fixed text, so a refusal never
tells a token's holder that the account was revoked. The access value gains `principal.suspended`
for a signed-in principal whose grants are on hold, past the grace period or after a failed exchange.
An application then serves a search the open tier and answers an export, a grant-only fetch, missing
objects included, and a write with 503. decisions.md records both with the alternatives weighed, and
an open-tier marker on a suspended principal's export is on the roadmap as a possible improvement.

## The database schema is drafted

`.dev/design/schema.md` turns the logical model into PostgreSQL tables and reads them against the
token calculation's steps, with the notice stream the bridge's test plan fixes for the controller.
Eight decisions await review, each with a recommendation: a principal's revocation as a history table
of its own, since grants a rule confers have no row to revoke; a group belonging to one resource,
enforced by a composite key from grants; the holder as two columns so it can carry foreign keys; a
null record category as the control-plane wildcard; category notices filtered by audience and principal notices tagged per audience;
Kysely as the data access layer; forward-only migrations; and a controller database role that cannot
alter the schema and holds append-only tables by privilege. Notice positions follow commit order by
serializing the writes under one advisory lock, and a poll from a position older than the stream
keeps answers "reset", which drops every cached entry. token-calculation.md's step 5 no longer gives
the adapter the clauses the bridge renders.

## Only a timely poll proves the notice stream is live

The open adversarial-delay item had a concrete cause: an open push connection counted as a
successful revocation check, and a connection held open while its events are delayed looks current.
A check now succeeds only when a poll is answered within its deadline, timed on the bridge's own
clock. Over TLS a pending notice rides in every answer, so holding back one person's notice holds
back every answer, and the grace period bounds the delay whatever the attack. Proposed for review in
`to-discuss.md`. The security workflow, the glossary and the bridge's test plan carry the rule, and
the time-based poll endpoint is replaced by the position-based one in `roadmap.md`,
`architecture.md` and `phase-1.md`.

## A catalogue's category gap is checked in the bridge, from what each resource offers

The check protecting `unmarked` from a category a catalogue neither maps nor declares absent had been
placed in the controller, which cannot withhold a resource from one catalogue, since a token covers
the whole application. It now runs in the bridge, reading a new payload member, `offeredCategories`,
computed with the grants. Proposed for review in `decisions.md`, with the alternatives. The payload
type, the fixtures and their checker, the token calculation's step 5 and the bridge's test plan carry
it, and the first application's adapter test plan now hands its declarations to the bridge rather
than checking a dictionary itself. Resources "offer" categories and records "carry" one, defined in
the glossary. Two passages left from the superseded resource-level decision, in `decisions.md` and
`phase-1.md`, are corrected.

## The erasure question is narrowed, and two audit items answered

The append-only log's conflict with erasure comes down to the email address carried in clear by
three invitation events, since the subject is already a pseudonym. A keyed digest is recommended,
and whether a pseudonymous trail outlives an erasure request is left as a legal question. A
controller instance's failure no longer puts its bridges into the uncertain mode, and migration access
to the audit table is answered by the schema draft's decision H, with a policy that migrations never
rewrite its rows.

## The bridge's research settles its contracts, and the schema gains applications

The bridge's research on the Usher token, the notice stream, the core and the sign-in precheck
answered what units 3 and 4 needed. Decided or proposed from it: the bridge never refuses a credential
the controller would accept, so it checks no sign-in signature, and a flood of junk credentials is
answered by the in-flight bound as an accepted trade, with admission control on the roadmap as
research; a change to the baseline drops every entry, since every signed-in payload carries the
baseline too; and an Usher token the bridge cannot read is treated as not received, `503`. Case 18
requires the controller to know which applications serve which resources, so the schema draft gains
`applications` and `application_resources`, step 5 filters by audience, and Decide E filters category
notices by audience and tags principal notices per audience. A cursor history no longer reaches
answers `410`, judged against a trim watermark, with an epoch that changes when history restarts. The
fixture checker gains the rules the validator shares: a name after a category's scope, no empty or
`__proto__` identifiers, and `generatedAt` no later than `iat`.
