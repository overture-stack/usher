# 2026-10-03

## The record is the unit of access, confirmed for iMicroSeq's first release

Content nested inside a record is part of what a reader is admitted to, while a reference to another
catalogue's record grants nothing beyond the reference: a file record listing its specimens'
identifiers shows those identifiers, and the specimens stay records of their own catalogue, reached
under that catalogue's grants. decisions.md records this beside the unit-of-access rule.
permissions-model.md's field-level section now says what field-level control is mainly for: withholding
part of a record's own nested content, and also a privileged flat value such as a government
identification number. Access that varies with each nested item's own value is a separate capability,
not designed, and the roadmap carries it as post-MVP research.

## The request's access is one value, carried in each framework's own store

adapter-integration.md now defines what a bridge hands an adapter for each request: one plain,
JSON-shaped value, `UsherRequestAccess`, holding a version, the principal (its subject or none, and
whether it holds only the open tier) and one enforcement result per registered key, and never the
token, payload or grants. `attachAccess` validates and freezes it under the key `usher`, and
adapters read it only through `readAccess`, which throws on a missing, invalid or unknown-version
value. Each bridge uses its framework's own per-request store, `res.locals` in Express, so the value
moves unchanged between bridges and, published as a JSON Schema, between languages. The glossary
defines the term.

## The bridge's test plan, and the grace period confirmed

`.dev/docs/bridge-tests.md` plans step 2: the exchange, token handling, rendering every case against
step 1's fixture rows, the cache and every way an entry is dropped, the degraded modes, the Express
layer and the log events, then step 1's suites rerun against the real bridge. Its controller mock
serves the channels as scripted events, and the contracts it serves are what the controller
implements and what the schema's channel half is designed from. Past the grace period the bridge
serves the open tier alone, marked open-tier only, and answers 503 only for what a grant alone could
answer; a resource closed during an outage staying open until the channel returns is accepted,
since data moves from closed to open far more often than the reverse. The statements that still said
503 before any read path are aligned, along with two that still had the adapter receiving the
payload.
