# 2026-10-05

## Notices travel as wake-ups, and the poll keeps its own position

Valkey pub/sub delivers at most once, so it now only wakes controller instances, which read new
notices from PostgreSQL and push them; a lost wake-up costs a delay, never a notice. The poll runs
whether or not the push is connected and asks after the last poll answer applied, which pushed
notices never move, so a notice one instance's push lost is still polled. `security-workflow.md`,
`architecture.md` and the technology stack now say so, and the stack's contradictory Streams sentence
is gone.

## The bridge's events are in the audit catalogue

Twelve bridge events join `audit-events.md` under its naming rules. The refusal and failure of an
exchange are `credential.refusal` and `token.exchangeFailure`, since no word may be both entity and
action, and the refusal's field is `origin`, since the envelope owns `source`. `category.unmapped`,
`catalogue.resourceListMissing` and `bridge.internalError` are `critical`, the severity logged at
error level. `category.unenforced` also covers a category entry carrying `field`, through a new
`entry` field beside its decided reason, and the mode change gains `cold` and a probable key mismatch.

## The bridge's research and usher-types land, and the corpus matches the validator

The five research notes are in the atlas under a Bridge heading, each leading with its decisions.
`usher-types` defines the payload, the enforcement result, the request's access value and the
canonical form, each validated by hand and failing closed, with 705 tests. The fixture checker now
refuses what the validator refuses: booleans and out-of-range timestamps, an empty `sub`, `iss` or
`aud`, unknown root members, a separator after a category's scope, and unscoped field categories.
decisions.md records the validator's stricter filter rules, among them no wildcard, no value Arranger
reads as an instruction, and a `not` only beside an `in`. The catalogue gains `bridge.startRefusal`,
and `token.overtaken` gains the outcome of a second overtaking.

## The category gap is checked in the bridge, decided, and an unconfirmed credential is suspended

The developer approved the bridge running the category-gap check from `offeredCategories`. The
adapter's job is unchanged: it turns its application's configuration into the one registration the
bridge reads, and the bridge compares that registration with what each resource offers, the same way
for every application. The request's access value now distinguishes a principal presenting no
credential, never suspended, from one whose credential the bridge has not confirmed, suspended with
a null subject and served the open tier, so the degraded modes can be represented.

## The category check's edge is written down, and a cache race is on record

An adversarial review of the bridge-side category check, adopted in full by the developer, leaves the
decision standing and sharpens it. The check compares what Usher declares with what a catalogue
registered and never reads the data, so a value mapped nowhere stays with the reconciliation
protocol; registration refuses a declared absence where a category field exists; a category is
mapped before any record carrying it is indexed; offered categories are enforcement input only; and
`category.unmapped` is counted per catalogue, category and resource. The review also found that a
payload computed before a revocation can be written to the controller's cache after the revocation
cleared it, reissuing the revoked grant indefinitely. It is on record as a HIGH item, with a policy
generation in the cache key proposed as the fix. Global category names are now unique in the schema
draft, since PostgreSQL otherwise counts their null resource as distinct.
